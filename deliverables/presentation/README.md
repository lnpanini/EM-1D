## 3. Is it a full-wave loop? — the sanity check, answered

Free-space λ₀ at 2.45 GHz = **122.4 mm**. The in-plane conductor path is
**123.34 mm**.

**L / λ₀ = 1.008.** The claim *"a loop resonates when its perimeter ≈ one
wavelength"* is **CONFIRMED** — this is a full-wave loop, and it is decisively
**not** a half-wave resonator (that would need ~61 mm, and the path is twice that).

But the *reason* on the slide is wrong. Solving for the ε_eff each hypothesis needs:

| | L used | L/λ₀ | ε_eff for full-wave | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| z-wave @ 2.174 GHz | in-plane 123.3 | 0.894 | **1.25** | plausible |
| z-wave @ 2.174 GHz | 3D 164.3 | 1.191 | 0.71 | impossible (<1) |
| flat control @ 2.620 GHz | in-plane 123.3 | 1.078 | 0.86 | impossible (<1) |

**ε_eff comes out ≈ 1, not 2.6.** A Ø0.5 mm wire in a 6.5 mm slab of ε_r 2.6 barely
loads — most of the field is in air and tissue. So the sentence to use is:

> *The meander folds one free-space wavelength of conductor (123 mm) into a 20 mm
> ring. The substrate contributes almost no wavelength shortening.*

**Two consequences you should be ready for in Q&A:**

- The 3D length (164.3 mm) is **not** the electrical length. If it were, the loop
  would resonate near 1.8 GHz; it resonates at 2.174.
- Comparing the flat control (in-plane 123.3 mm → 2.620 GHz) with the z-wave (same
  in-plane, 3D 164.3 mm → 2.174 GHz) gives the electrically realised fraction of the
  out-of-plane length: **α = 0.62**. That independently reproduces the 0.60–0.70
  reported in `docs/ZWAVE-STRAIN-FINDINGS.md`, from a completely different route.

## 4. Path length vs strain — the mechanism table

| Strain | Z-wave L (3D) | ΔL/L | Flat L | ΔL/L | Δf predicted by *f* ∝ 1/L |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 % | 154.29 mm | — | 113.37 mm | — | — |
| 5 % | 153.87 | −0.27 % | 114.91 | +1.36 % | z-wave +0.27 %, flat −1.34 % |
| 10 % | 153.75 | −0.35 % | 116.61 | +2.86 % | z-wave +0.35 %, flat −2.78 % |
| 15 % | 153.91 | −0.25 % | 118.47 | +4.50 % | z-wave +0.25 %, flat −4.31 % |
| 20 % | 154.30 | **+0.01 %** | 120.45 | **+6.25 %** | z-wave −0.01 %, **flat −5.88 %** |

This is the figure that proves the mechanism: **the z-wave's conductor length is
constant to 0.01 % at 20 % strain** while the flat loop's grows 6.25 %.

**It also resolves the −5.7 % / −7.82 % contradiction.** They are different
quantities, and both are correct:

- **−5.7 %** = the *arc-length prediction* for the **flat** loop at 20 % strain
  (reproduced here as −5.88 %). Path length only.
- **−10.38 %** = the *full-wave measured* drift of the flat control. Nearly double,
  because path growth is only about half the story — substrate thinning also pulls
  the loop toward the body.
- **−7.82 %** = the *full-wave measured* drift of the **z-wave**.

So the deck's "+6 % path → −5.7 %" sentence is right **about the flat design's path
term** and must not be presented as the z-wave's result.

## 6. Where the power goes — item 29 answered, and not as expected

`scripts/loss_budget.py`, at 2.44 GHz, as a share of **accepted** power:

| Channel | Share |
|---|---|
| **Radiated** | **5.22 %** |
| Muscle | 37.03 % |
| Skin | 24.52 % |
| Fat | 5.24 % |
| **Tissue total** | **66.79 %** |
| **Ecoflex dielectric (tan δ)** | **23.34 %** |
| **EGaIn conductor** | **4.65 %** |

**Your expectation was that EGaIn's conductivity would matter most. It matters
least.** Of the two antenna-material loss channels, **Ecoflex dielectric loss beats
EGaIn conductor loss by 5×** (23.3 % vs 4.65 %) — and both are dwarfed by tissue
absorption at 66.8 %.

Three things follow, and they are all slide-worthy:

1. **This antenna is absorption-limited, not conductor-limited.** Swapping EGaIn for
   copper would recover at most ~4.4 points of the 95 % that does not radiate.
   Arguing about EGaIn's 17×-worse conductivity is arguing about the smallest term.
2. **The 5.22 % radiation efficiency is mostly physics, not a design failure.** Any
   antenna 4.25 mm from muscle at 2.45 GHz pays this. It is worth saying out loud
   before someone asks why the number is so low.
3. ⚠️ **The most consequential unverified number in the whole model is `tan δ`.** It
   controls the second-largest loss channel, the model uses **0.03**, your deck says
   **0.015**, and neither is cited. If 0.015 is right, dielectric loss roughly halves
   and radiation efficiency rises materially. **Get a real reference for this before
   presenting any efficiency number.**
